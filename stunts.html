Stunts and scoring guide

Updated 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Distance keeps your score ticking over, but stunts are where the big numbers come from. A Perfect triple kick flip landed on a full ×8 chain is worth 21,600 points on its own. This guide lists exactly what every stunt pays and how to pull each one off.

How your score adds up

Your score is the sum of three things:

  • Distance: one point for every metre you travel.
  • Mach diamonds: 25 points each. Diamonds are not multiplied, and each one is also banked as a credit for the Garage.
  • Stunt bonuses: the points below, multiplied by your landing grade and by your current chain.

Landing grades

At touchdown the game measures how far the car is from level and grades the landing:

  • Perfect, within 10° of level: 1.5× points.
  • Clean, within 28°: full points.
  • Sketchy, within 50°: half points, and the car bounces once and loses a fifth of its speed. The bounce gives you a moment to level out before it settles, but a second bad touchdown can still roll you.
  • Rolled over, beyond 50°: the run ends. Past 120° the game calls it "Landed on the roof".

Spinning counts against you. If the car is still rotating faster than about 230° per second when the wheels touch, extra degrees are added to the measurement, up to about 18° at full spin. Stopping the rotation before you land matters as much as the angle itself.

How to kick-flip

  1. Get airborne. Any ramp works, or hold THRUST on flat ground to hop.
  2. Hold FLIP straight away (F, S, ↓, Shift or the FLIP button). The car barrel-rolls to the left and speeds up to its maximum spin of about 490° per second. The first flip takes a little under a second; each extra flip takes about three-quarters of a second more.
  3. Watch the count. In the air the readout shows ROLL and the number of flips completed. A flip is credited once the car has turned about 326°, so the last few degrees can be part of levelling out.
  4. Let go early. The spin carries on after you release FLIP and only fades slowly.
  5. Counter the spin. Press right (→, D or ▶), the opposite way to the flip. Stopping a full-speed spin takes about 0.4 seconds and roughly 90 to 100 degrees of rotation, so start countering about a quarter-turn before level.
  6. Settle it. Once the gyroscope turns cyan, switch to small taps until the wheels touch down.

Plan around your hang time. Early in a run, a lineup jump keeps you in the air for about a second and a half, which is enough for a single flip with time to recover. Double and triple flips need the higher launches you get at speed later in the run, or a burst of THRUST for extra hang time. Holding THRUST also steadies the car slightly while the lift jets fire.

The Ramp, step by step

The Ramp is the tall concrete curl from the reveal: a ribbon that rises from the floor, runs vertical and curls over until the car hangs upside down about 67 ft (20 m) up. The first one stands in the right lane at the start of every run. Later ones can appear once you have driven 1.1 km since the last, announced by a "THE RAMP →" sign about 120 metres before the base.

  1. Line up. A line of six diamonds leads into the right lane. Be in that lane and on your wheels when you reach the base: suction only grabs a car that arrives below about 13 ft. Fly into the ramp higher than that and you hit the tower.
  2. Let the fans work. The car climbs by itself, slowing to a crawl as it goes over the top. The DOWNFORCE readout climbs from about 2,500 to 4,700 kg, and your air tank tops up a little on the way.
  3. Hang. At the top the car hangs upside down for just over a second.
  4. Release. When RELEASE flashes, hold THRUST immediately. The jets slow the fall to a gentle descent of about 10 mph and swing the car from upside down back to level in about a second and a half. Without thrust it barely rotates and drops like a stone.
  5. Ride the jets down. Keep THRUST held all the way to the ground, about four to five seconds and roughly 300 litres of air. Balance the roll with left and right. You cannot kick-flip during the descent.
  6. Touch down. Land within 12° of level and slower than about 13 mph for a PERFECT DESCENT worth 2,000. Within 50° and under about 34 mph is a SAFE LANDING worth 1,200. Anything worse ends the run.

After you land, launch control fires again for another 0–60 mph in under a second, and the car steers itself out of the ramp's lane back to the centre.

Arriving at the base with an almost empty tank is the most common way to fail The Ramp. Avoid long burns in the last few hundred metres before it.

Near misses and roof runs

A near miss is scored when you pass a parked vehicle with less than about 0.9 m to spare, either beside it on the ground or just over its roof in the air. Barriers do not count. Parked weaves and lineups are the best places to collect them.

A roof run is scored when you land on top of a semi truck: 200 points per semi. Roof-run sections line up two semis nose to tail after a narrow ramp in the centre lane, so one clean jump can pay 400 points plus ten diamonds along the roofs. Pickups, sedans and robotaxis are solid too and you can land on them, but only semis pay the roof-run bonus.

Chains

Every stunt landed within four seconds of the previous one raises your chain multiplier by one, up to ×8, and the multiplier applies to that stunt's points. Four seconds without a stunt resets it. Diamonds neither add to a chain nor break it.

A typical chain: skim a parked car on the ground (75 × 1 = 75), launch off a lineup ramp two seconds later and land a Perfect kick flip (600 × 2 = 1,200), then clear the next car's roof by a whisker (75 × 3 = 225). Keep the stunts coming and the multiplier makes every flip worth several times its base value.
